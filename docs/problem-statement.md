Allymon is a Toronto fintech trying to launch a peer-to-peer lending platform, and they need something to actually test loan risk before they build the real thing. That's where this project comes in. We're building a proof-of-concept Loan Risk Engine that takes a synthetic loan application, checks it against a mock credit bureau, and spits out a decision: Approve, Deny, or Manual Review. Every decision has to come with reasons attached, not just a number. If I were a borrower getting denied, I'd want to know why, not just get a black-box "no."

A few things really matter here. The system needs to respond in under 3 seconds, which isn't really that hard for a rules engine, but it still has to hold up under load. Uptime needs to stay at 99% or better, and I don't think that's negotiable for anything that's supposed to look like a real financial product. Every request also needs full audit logging, so if something goes wrong later, we can actually trace it back.

None of this touches real data ever. It's all synthetic which is the whole point of a safe proof of concept. There's no real credit bureau involved either, just the mock one the course gives us, so nothing here is pulling actual financial data on anyone.
